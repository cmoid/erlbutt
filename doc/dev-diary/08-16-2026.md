## [erlbutt](https://github.com/cmoid/erlbutt)

This week I spent mostly testing, I'm building some new common tests that will simulate a real collection of nodes replicating.

I've also been thinking a lot about the problem of on-boarding. I think the two major issues are 

1.The feeds get too long, and 

2. we replicate too many of them. 

Social network graphs seem to be very highly connected locally so any small hop count can  quickly result in thousands of feeds, most of which a typical user will ignore, given Dunbar's number.

Archiving helps. One doesn't need to replicate the archive, though it's necessary to trully validate the entire feed. But the archive is part of a signed new nil post so one can also accept it as is. Erlbutt also stores a hint file, a hack I got from Bitcask, that supports fast lookups of messages in an archive. So this helps with 1.

For 2. I was thinking a simple idea is to exploit the notion of derived versus foundational truth. Every index we build from a feed or a stream of messages can always be re-derived from the feed. Together with the secret the feed is the absolute truth. What I imagine we can do is consider the boundary of a region of the follow graph based on the hop count, as a special region where we don't replicate feeds, but instead replicate something like a *feed manifest*. This could be the derived state of the profile, a small collection of sample posts from the feed, or whatever a user wishes to share as a representation. It would be signed.

Signing provides some validation but it's not complete. What we could add to these manifests is a trust weight that originates with the user doing the replication. It might be based on distance from the user, or level of trust in the direct follows, or anything for that matter. What this provides is a view of additional feeds without paying the full price of replicating and validating. Users could browse these. For any of interest they could then replicated the entire feed and recompute the manifest to verify the *derived* truth.

If this idea holds up after I recover from jet lag I'm going to implement a prototype of it in both #erlbutt and #silkpurse. I'll perhaps do it by adding some optionals args to replication requests so as to keep it backwards compatible.

There's real beauty in the simple `SSB` feed. I think recognizing the distinction between ground and derived truth can go a long way to giving us simple solutions to some of these problems.
