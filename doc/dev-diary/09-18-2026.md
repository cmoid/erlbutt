## [erlbutt](https://github.com/cmoid/erlbutt)

It's been a bit slow for me the last few weeks. I did spend some time working on what I call *feed glimpses* and wrote this [design doc](https://github.com/cmoid/erlbutt/blob/main/doc/research/feed-glimpses.md). I'm going to take a first pass at implementing it this week. 

After that I'm going to implement an exporter, so that peers can export their feed and blobs. Your data is not sovereign if it's not portable. There's a bit to figure out there as I write these hint files with the archives that enable fast lookups. They are tied to the `SSB` log format so that needs to be more portable. I was thinking the [sneakerweb](https://sneakerweb.org) work could be used, at least for the packaging, the willow data model differs too much from `SSB` to be usable.

Beyond these last features, I'm going to switch to focusing on the use case I've always had in mind for `SSB`, `EHR` (medical records). I see this as just another app, similar to the way silkpurse has an app that plugs into erlbutt. 

## [silkpurse](https://github.com/cmoid/silkpurse/tree/theseus)

The plan is to enhance silkpurse (both the patchwork clone and the erlbutt app), to surface feed glimpses, so we can see it in action. Similar to feed archiving these features will never be very useful unless more clients implement them. I'm just curious if these new features solve the on-boarding problem.

I also need to create some much larger multi-node common tests in order to quantify the benefit from archiving. I've had a great experience with silkpurse. It's not heavy on features but it's fast and I can readily switch betwen EarlButt and Moid using the same client.

## [The Commonwealth of Scuttlebutt](%gUxXVdV04PVya17jAa19Kar70fxUVHOs7T/0qkrIFtc=.sha256)

A good non-profit requires three things:

  * vision
  * mission
  * duty of care

  I think many butts agree on the first, a more decentralized P2P world where our data is sovereign and we share what we want when we want and can choose not to suffer fools. Back when a bunch of work was done on listing values, I found I could get on board with many, if not all of them.

  Mission is more difficult but without it, visions are just dreams. `SSB` has been pretty fragmented over the years, I think it's just a characteristic of open source projects. This is where companies have an advantage. Communities of volunteers don't typically have the same level of committment that comes with a labor contract. I also think the donation model as a way of creating open source sustainability is flawed. Donations always follow a power curve. That's mainly why I closed my open collective account. I also don't think busking is a good way for programmers to make the rent, it doesn't work very well for music either. So what's the mission of `SSB`?

  The duty of care is trivial, it's just obeying the law you live under, it's simple, you can hire people who do it for a living. This is the one that many dot orgs fail at though. Public benefit corps. are interesting but a little opaque. There's this committment to do good things but they are still controlled by groups of inside shareholders. It's a smell.
